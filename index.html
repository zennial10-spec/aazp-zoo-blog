About AAZP

Arignar Anna Zoological Park (AAZP), popularly known as Vandalur Zoo, is one of the largest and most scientifically managed zoological parks in India. Spread across 602 hectares of protected reserve forest in Vandalur, Chennai, Tamil Nadu, the park was established in 1855 as the Madras Zoo — making it the first public zoo in India. In 1985, it was relocated to its current expansive campus within the Vandalur Reserve Forest, and renamed in honor of former Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Arignar Anna.

Today, AAZP is home to over 2,500 animals across 170+ species of mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish. The park operates under the Central Zoo Authority (CZA) and is recognized as a "Large Category Zoo" for its scientific management, conservation breeding programs, and research initiatives. Its naturalistic enclosures are designed to mimic native habitats, spanning open moated exhibits, walk-through aviaries, nocturnal houses, and specialized breeding centers.

AAZP is a global leader in ex-situ conservation, participating in coordinated breeding programs for critically endangered species including the Lion-tailed Macaque, Nilgiri Langur, Bengal Tiger, Indian Rhinoceros, Great Indian Bustard, and Gharial. The park's veterinary hospital, rescue and rehabilitation center, and conservation research laboratory support wildlife health, disease surveillance, and reintroduction efforts. As an education hub, AAZP welcomes over 2 million visitors annually and runs nature camps, zoo schools, and outreach programs to inspire conservation awareness.

Reflection

Thoughts on wildlife, conservation, and the role of zoological parks in education and species preservation.

"Walking through Arignar Anna Zoological Park, I was reminded that every enclosure represents not just an animal on display, but a species with a complex history, a fragile present, and an uncertain future. The park's work in conservation breeding — particularly for endemic Western Ghats species like the Lion-tailed Macaque and Nilgiri Langur — demonstrates how modern zoos have evolved from mere menageries into vital arks for biodiversity."
— Personal reflection, September 2026

What I Learned

  • Zoos play a critical role in ex-situ conservation and species recovery programs
  • The Western Ghats is a biodiversity hotspot with many endemic species under threat
  • Habitat loss and human-wildlife conflict are primary drivers of population decline
  • Public education through direct observation fosters conservation awareness
